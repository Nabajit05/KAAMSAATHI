2. WORKER NEEDS AND PRODUCT BOUNDARIES

Primary users have limited time and may use inexpensive Android phones, mixed Hindi/English, shared devices and unreliable mobile data. They may have screenshots rather than downloadable statements. They may be worried about lost earnings and distrust unexplained automated decisions.

Design for three situations:
A. “I completed the incentive target but received less than expected.”
B. “I see a deduction or settlement difference I do not understand.”
C. “I worked for a contractor and need a record of what is due.”

The system provides preparation and explanation. It cannot force a platform or contractor to pay, prove facts absent from evidence, determine legal liability, guarantee welfare eligibility or reverse account deactivation.

Research-informed decisions:
- Fairwork India 2024 found only two of eleven assessed platforms evidenced its minimum-wage-after-costs policy standard. This is not proof that every other platform underpays every worker.
- The GigSaathi research pilot already used earnings screenshots. It also reported low participation and insufficient immediate value. Our differentiation must be useful checking and case preparation in the first session.
- Worker organizations already help resolve disputes. Prepare clear evidence for existing support and human assistance channels.
- Welfare discovery is already served by myScheme and Haqdarshak. Our lightweight welfare feature explains verified next steps, rather than claiming to reinvent national scheme discovery.
- Real-world recovery rates and the frequency of independently checkable payout errors remain unvalidated. Never display invented success rates, testimonials, recovered-money counters or partnerships.

Build one coherent core journey, with small functional supporting paths. Priority determines implementation order, not permission to silently remove requirements.

P0 — complete and verify first:

P1 — narrow complete implementations immediately after the core:

- A curated Help area for rights/process guidance, safety practices and welfare awareness, in both languages, with sources and review dates.

P2 — only after the acceptance gates:

Alignment:
- Earnings tracking: explain what was earned, adjusted, paid and spent, without double counting.
- Multilingual assistance: allow workers to understand and correct every consequential fact.
- Document assistance: extract, explain, preserve and organize worker-supplied records.
- Grievance drafting: create a factual, evidence-linked complaint the worker reviews and submits.
- Rights guidance: explain verified relevant processes, uncertainties and human assistance routes.
- Welfare awareness: provide a small set of verified official pathways without guaranteeing eligibility.
- Safety: provide short, source-backed, non-diagnostic safety guidance and an emergency-help route that never depends on an AI response.

Every feature must answer a concrete worker need. No social feed, gamification, streaks, “AI agent” marketing panels, automatic legal notices, loans, leaderboards, earnings predictions or engagement notifications unrelated to a case.

4. VISUAL DESIGN AND ACCESSIBILITY

Before implementing, inspect available official design references briefly. Record three borrowed interaction principles, not a long moodboard. Do not copy logos, branded screens, proprietary illustrations or trade dress.

References and adaptations:
- GOV.UK Design System question pages: one question at a time, only necessary fields, clear back/continue controls, “I don’t know,” preserved answers and useful error recovery.
- Material Design 3 navigation and progress patterns: familiar mobile controls, clearly labeled destinations and recognizable states. Use official documentation if available; do not claim you inspected it if you could not.
- WCAG 2.2 AA: use its requirements as the accessibility target and verify the implemented journeys. Do not equate automated checks with full conformance.

Visual direction:
- Calm, trustworthy and modern. Warm light background, white surfaces, dark charcoal text and deep teal for primary actions. Use amber for “needs attention”; red only for actual errors/destructive actions.
- Proposed tokens: background #F7F8F5, surface #FFFFFF, text #172B2A, primary #0F665D, border #D5DEDA. Measure contrast in the actual combinations before finalizing; proposed colors are not pre-certified.
- Main content uses a 16px minimum body size, generous line height, 8px spacing rhythm and approximately 16px side gutters on small phones. Support Hindi glyphs and use tabular numerals for money when available.
- Main controls should be at least 48 CSS pixels high where practical. Maintain WCAG target-size and spacing requirements throughout.
- No decorative hero image, large video, animated gradients, glass effects, dark-pattern countdowns or expensive icon libraries. Use a simple text wordmark and a few familiar SVG icons.
- Design for 360px first; verify 320px, 390px, tablet and desktop. Do not force a desktop dashboard into mobile.
- On desktop, use an evidence/result two-column layout where useful. The worker should not have to scan a sidebar full of features.

Accessibility requirements:
- Semantic HTML, proper heading order, persistent labels, descriptive button names and labeled icons.
- Keyboard operation, visible focus, appropriate dialog focus management, escape behavior and focus restoration.
- Input errors connected with aria-describedby; an error summary linking to invalid inputs when needed.
- Announce upload, extraction and save status with restrained live regions. Do not read every streaming token aloud.
- Never communicate a result only through color or an icon.
- Support 200% zoom, 320px reflow, reduced motion and system text scaling.
- Recording has start, stop, cancel and duration controls; no press-and-hold requirement. Audio has a text equivalent and never autoplays.
- OTP supports paste and appropriate autocomplete; do not impose memory puzzles or prevent password-manager behavior.
- Proper page/fragment language attributes; format Indian currency and dates consistently. Human-readable Hindi, not overly formal literal translation.
- Essential content must remain selectable text. Test Hindi rendering in printable/exported output.

5. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE AND COMPLETE USER JOURNEY

Use four clearly labeled primary destinations: Home, Earnings, Cases and Help. Language and account/privacy settings live in the header/account area. Give “Check my payment” the dominant Home action. Hide the bottom navigation during focused steps when it would distract; preserve a clear exit/save path.

Screen A — Welcome and demo
- Explain the service in one sentence and three short actions: add payment records, check the details, prepare a complaint if needed.
- Allow language selection before authentication.
- Offer “Try an example” using exclusively fictional data. Label sample screens visibly.
- A real private case requires authenticated ownership before transmitting worker records. Do not require an account for reading public Help or trying a sample.
- No Aadhaar, bank password, UPI PIN, platform password or unnecessary full bank statement.

Screen B — Authentication and concise consent
- Prefer a managed phone OTP flow if an SMS provider is configured; never simulate SMS delivery as real authentication.
- If unavailable, ask before switching to a different real login method. Synthetic demo mode can proceed independently.
- Explain who processes uploaded data and why, in plain language. Separate the choice to keep a draft on the current device from server storage.
- Offer “This is a shared phone” and default to not retaining private drafts locally.

Screen C — Home
- One dominant action: “Check my payment.”
- Secondary action: “Add earnings.”
- Show at most one recent case/next action, if present.
- A small “Need help?” entry leads to Help. Empty state invites the first useful action.
- No fabricated personalized totals and no giant analytics grid.

Screen D — Issue selection and intake
- Choose delivery/platform work or daily-wage work.
- Platform issues: missing incentive, deduction I do not understand, payout does not match, or not sure.
- Ask for platform and pay period only when relevant. Use the configured market timezone and explicitly capture any source-period timezone differences.
- Record a short explanation or type it. Transcribe to editable text and ask the worker to confirm significant facts.
- Do not require a detailed personal profile. State/location is requested only for jurisdiction-specific guidance.

Screen E — Add evidence
- Show a screenshot example explaining what to include: full incentive conditions, activity for the same period, and payout breakdown.
- Accept the supported image formats; use clear file count/size limits. Proposed MVP: JPEG, PNG and WebP, up to five images, 8 MB each, with server enforcement. PDF support is optional until securely parsed and tested; do not accept it with a dead implementation.
- Provide camera and gallery upload, preview, remove, replace and per-file status.
- Preserve the submitted original. If the worker explicitly redacts before upload, keep that version as the submitted evidence and explain this distinction. Never call a generated or modified image an original platform record.
- Strip location-bearing metadata from the AI-processing derivative, not silently from the retained evidence. Disclose any original metadata retained and never treat metadata as proof of authenticity.
- Permit manual confirmed entry when upload is unavailable; label its provenance as worker-entered and lower the conclusion strength accordingly.

Screen F — Review extracted facts
- Display human labels, not raw JSON. Every extracted amount/date/condition links to its image or quoted source fragment.
- Ask “Is this correct?” with edit actions. Highlight conflicts and unreadable fields.
- Critical amounts, periods and qualifying conditions require confirmation before checking.
- Ask one missing question at a time. Stop asking if the answer cannot be known; explain which document would help.

Screen G — Check result
- Three outcomes: “The records match,” “Possible payment difference,” and “More information needed.”
- Show observed expected amount, observed paid amount and difference only when the evidence supports the comparison.
- Put the most important reason first, with expandable “How we checked” arithmetic and evidence links.
- Clearly identify verified-from-records, worker-stated and unknown facts. Do not show a model-generated confidence percentage as if it were calibrated certainty.
- Use “Based on the records you confirmed…” rather than “The company owes you…”
- If missing evidence blocks a conclusion, make “Add the missing record” primary.
- Offer relevant sourced process guidance. Do not make a legal determination.

Screen H — Complaint review and export
- Draft a respectful complaint containing the period, issue, observed figures, supporting references and requested clarification/correction.
- Allow editing, language switching and short audio playback if supported. Preserve identifiers and amounts during translation.
- Link every material allegation to confirmed facts. Unsupported details must be omitted or explicitly presented as the worker’s account.
- Worker reviews and chooses copy, device share or printable summary. Never send automatically.
- “Open support instructions” uses a verified route. Do not invent support emails or assume a consumer grievance portal applies to a worker dispute.
- An export is not a submission. The worker may later record “I submitted it,” ticket ID and date.

Screen I — Case detail and follow-up
- Show a timeline with draft created, records checked, complaint prepared, worker-reported submission and worker-reported outcome.
- Keep calculation outcome separate from case workflow status.
- “Resolved” and “amount received” are worker-reported unless independently evidenced; never update them from a model guess.
- Permit another record or response to be added; changes invalidate stale calculations and complaint approvals.
- Support user-chosen follow-up dates without implying a statutory deadline. No automated outbound messaging in this MVP.

Screen J — Earnings
- Platform mode: record period, platform, gross earnings, adjustments, settlement/cash details and expenses. Mark missing values as unknown, not zero.
- Distinguish payout amount from earnings and spendable income. Use the domain model below.
- Daily-wage mode: record agreed rate, days or half-days worked, advances, payments and balance. Clearly distinguish a worker’s statement from corroborated records.
- Show a simple period summary and list. No complicated charts are necessary.
- Explain estimates, missing expenses and overlaps. Do not produce a misleading hourly rate from incomplete hours or cross-platform double-counted time.

Screen K — Help
- Three sections: Payment and complaint help; Safety; Welfare and documents.
- Seed only a small set of current, verified official/process resources appropriate to the chosen jurisdiction.
- Each item contains a short explanation, practical next step, source link and last-reviewed date.
- Welfare: explain registration versus benefit entitlement; do not claim e-Shram registration automatically guarantees cash or health coverage. Link to the current official process and show uncertainty when eligibility cannot be assessed.
- Safety: concise road/heat/fatigue guidance grounded in appropriate authoritative material; no diagnosis, reassurance that continuing work is safe, or recommendations to speed to earn incentives. Verify emergency contact details before displaying them. Emergency access must remain usable when AI is unavailable.
- Rights: explain evidence preservation, seeking clarification, relevant appeal procedures and verified human assistance routes. Do not invent legal deadlines or universal legal entitlements.
- If a reliable source cannot be verified, exclude the claim or label the process as needing human verification; do not fill the gap with model memory.

Screen L — Privacy and settings
- Language, audio preference, shared-device setting, download my data, delete a case and delete my account/data.
- Explain data collected, purpose, processors, retention, deletion limits and how to get help.
- Destructive actions require clear confirmation. No hidden public sharing.

6. PAYMENT LOGIC — KEEP IT OUT OF THE LANGUAGE MODEL

Use integer paise for INR; reject unsafe ranges, invalid numeric formats and unsupported currencies. Store money semantics explicitly. Normalize localized numerals and comma formats deliberately and test them. Do not do monetary arithmetic with binary floating point.

Separate these concepts:
- Gross work earnings: base pay plus earned incentives and tips where applicable.
- Earnings adjustments: actual additions or deductions affecting earnings.
- Settlement offsets: cash already retained, advances, prior-period adjustments or other correctly classified offsets. An advance is not automatically a loss of earned income.
- Expected bank settlement: reconcile gross earnings, earnings adjustments and documented settlement offsets for the same period.
- Observed settlement: actual bank/platform settlement record, including pending status and payout cutoff.
- Net after recorded operating expenses: earnings after applicable adjustments minus separately recorded fuel/rental/other work costs; label incomplete expense data. Never subtract fuel from expected platform payout unless it is actually a documented platform deduction.

Maintain a signed, documented formula for each supported adapter. Unknown classifications must block definitive reconciliation. Prefer a narrow correct parser over claiming support for all platforms.

Incentive rules must explicitly model:
- Offer effective date and period/timezone.
- Qualifying completed order count and exclusions.
- Any acceptance/cancellation/attendance/zone/shift conditions present in the actual offer.
- Thresholds, whether tiers stack or only the highest tier applies, caps, payout timing and already credited incentive.
- Which conditions have evidence, which are worker-confirmed and which remain unknown.

Use three-valued eligibility: met, not_met, unknown. A missing condition is unknown, never automatically met. A mismatch is a possible discrepancy, not legal proof of debt.

Checks must handle overlapping periods, duplicate screenshots, multiple payout components, cash-on-delivery offsets, tips outside the platform, advance recovery, negative adjustments, partial settlements, previous-week adjustments, pending payouts and amended offers. Unsupported situations produce a specific explanation and request for evidence.

Daily-wage balances use agreed rate × confirmed units of work minus confirmed payments, with documented adjustments only. Unknown workdays cannot become confirmed because the model inferred a plausible number.

Record the rule version, source/input revision and arithmetic explanation for every result. Editing a critical fact makes previous results and drafts stale until rechecked.

7. AI ARCHITECTURE, GROUNDING AND COST CONTROL

GenAI must do real work: interpret images and voice, identify missing information, explain results and draft complaints. Arithmetic, permissions, state transitions and eligibility rules remain ordinary validated code.

Pipeline:
1. Authenticate and authorize the case; validate upload metadata and file bytes.
2. Prepare bounded image derivatives and compute evidence hashes. Avoid destructive downsampling of small payout text.
3. Make one structured extraction request for the new/revised evidence batch where feasible.
4. Validate output against shared schemas. Record source provenance and unknowns. Do not trust schema compliance as factual correctness.
5. Collect worker confirmation and missing answers.
6. Run the deterministic check.
7. On user request, generate one grounded explanation/complaint using confirmed facts, calculation output and the relevant retrieved source snippets.
8. Validate generated amounts, dates, identifiers and citation IDs against the supplied facts. Block or repair unsupported output once; then fall back to a deterministic factual template or an actionable error.
9. Generate speech only on demand, from the approved short text.

Suggested structured extraction fields:
- document_id, document_type, platform_as_written, pay_period_start/end, source_timezone;
- observations containing field_name, raw_text, normalized_value, unit/currency, source_document_id, page/image reference, source_excerpt and review_state;
- incentive_conditions with source excerpts and status unknown until checked;
- unreadable_fields, conflicting_observations and missing_information.
Represent unknown values with null and reasons. Do not fabricate bounding boxes; only show highlights if valid coordinates were actually returned and checked.

Security and quality:
- Uploaded content is untrusted data. Ignore instructions embedded in images, transcripts or documents. It must never authorize tools, change system behavior or redirect secret data.
- No autonomous email, browser navigation, arbitrary URL fetch, account access, money movement or complaint submission.
- Retrieved guidance must come from a curated, versioned allowlist. Treat retrieved text as data too. Escape output; do not render unsanitized model HTML.
- Separate worker allegations from observations in records. Use neutral wording.
- Do not calculate legal compensation, taxes or guaranteed benefits unless explicitly supported by reviewed deterministic rules, which are outside the initial MVP.
- Provider keys stay server-side. Pin a currently supported model identifier in configuration after checking its official documentation; do not invent a model name.
- Maintain small AI adapter interfaces for extraction, drafting, transcription and optional speech. Do not create an elaborate multi-agent system.

Efficiency:
- Hash evidence and cache extraction per owner/case, content version, schema/model version. Never share private cached results across users.
- Reuse confirmed facts; do not retransmit all documents on every step or language change.
- No AI calls on component renders, every keystroke, case list refreshes or deterministic calculations.
- Use bounded inputs, output-token caps, timeouts, per-user quotas and a project spending limit. Make provider failure visible.
- Permit at most one bounded corrective retry for invalid model output. Retry network/rate-limit failures with backoff only when safe and within the overall deadline.
- Coalesce duplicate requests using a server-side idempotency key and operation record. A repeated tap must not launch another billed extraction job.
- Do not put sensitive document contents into analytics, error reports or ordinary request logs.

Use official current documentation and the repository lockfile. Pin compatible dependencies; do not blindly upgrade everything to latest. Minimize dependencies, avoid microservices, custom authentication, vector databases for a handful of sources, and infrastructure requiring long approval cycles.

9. DATABASE, AUTHORIZATION AND API CONTRACTS

Use a small relational schema with explicit ownership and revisions. A practical starting model:
- profiles: user_id, preferred_locale, optional display_name, shared_device preference, consent version/timestamps.
- cases: id, owner_id, work_type, platform label, period, timezone, issue_type, workflow_status, current_input_revision, timestamps.
- evidence: id, case_id, owner_id, private storage key, submitted filename, verified MIME, byte_size, content hash, processing status and deletion state.
- case_facts: case_id, owner_id, revision, schema_version, validated structured observations, worker confirmations and source links. JSONB is acceptable with server-side schema validation.
- check_runs: id, case_id, owner_id, input_revision, rule_version, outcome, structured amounts/unknowns, explanation data and timestamp.
- complaint_drafts: id, case_id, owner_id, check_run_id, locale, editable text, revision, approval timestamp and export timestamp.
- earnings_entries: id, owner_id, optional case_id, work_type, period, source, structured money/work-unit components and provenance.
- case_events: id, case_id, owner_id, event_type, actor, bounded metadata and timestamp.
- guidance_sources: id, jurisdiction, category, official URL, title, reviewed_at, effective dates where known, source snapshot/hash and reviewed bilingual content.
- operations: idempotency_key, owner_id, case_id, input_revision, operation_type, status, error_code and timestamps for bounded resumable processing.

Combine tables only where that makes implementation simpler without losing ownership, revisions or auditability. Add foreign keys and indexes for owner/case/date queries. Avoid writing an elaborate framework.

Authorization:
- Verify the managed session/JWT server-side for every private operation.
- Derive owner_id from the verified identity, never from a trusted request body.
- Enforce row-level policies on every user-owned table and private storage path; use WITH CHECK for inserts/updates.
- Child rows must belong to a case owned by the same user. Cross-owner foreign-key references must be rejected by database constraints/policies and server checks.
- Public guidance can be read publicly but written only through a controlled administrative/deployment path.
- Use user-scoped database access where possible. Any server service-role client bypassing RLS must enforce equivalent ownership explicitly and is never exposed to the browser.
- Signed file URLs require ownership checks, short lifetimes and no public indexing.

Suggested endpoints; adapt naming to the framework:
- POST /api/cases; GET /api/cases; GET/PATCH/DELETE /api/cases/:id.
- POST /api/cases/:id/evidence/init; POST /api/cases/:id/evidence/:evidenceId/complete; DELETE /api/cases/:id/evidence/:evidenceId.
- POST /api/cases/:id/extract; GET /api/cases/:id/operations/:operationId.
- PATCH /api/cases/:id/facts with expected revision.
- POST /api/cases/:id/check with expected revision and idempotency key.
- POST /api/cases/:id/complaint; PATCH draft; GET export.
- POST /api/cases/:id/events for allowed worker-reported events only.
- GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/earnings as appropriate.
- GET /api/guidance with allowlisted filters.
- POST /api/speech/transcribe and optional /api/speech/synthesize with limits.
- GET /api/account/export; DELETE /api/account after reauthentication/confirmation.
- GET /api/health returning no secrets or private diagnostic data.

Validate on client for usability and server for trust. Return consistent safe errors containing a stable code, localized message, retryable flag, request ID and optional field errors. Use correct HTTP status codes. Never leak stack traces, provider payloads or whether another user’s case exists.

Bound concurrency and use revision checks so an old extraction cannot overwrite newer edits. Treat storage plus database changes as a recoverable workflow: clean orphan uploads, track partial deletion and never announce completion before all required steps succeed.

10. SECURITY AND DATA PRIVACY

Treat payment screenshots, phone numbers, audio, identifiers and complaints as private user data.

Mandatory controls:
- HTTPS in deployment. Secure managed session handling following the framework/provider guidance; secure cookie settings where cookies are used. CSRF protection for cookie-authenticated mutations and strict origin checks. Do not claim cookies are HttpOnly unless the actual integration implements this.
- Rate-limit OTP requests, authentication attempts, uploads, transcription and AI operations. Configure provider abuse protection; never hardcode a universal OTP or bypass verification in the live environment.
- Validate file signatures, MIME, size and dimensions server-side; reject active formats and unsupported input. Bound decoder resources and image pixel counts. Use random storage names and private buckets.
- Verify a direct-to-storage upload belongs to the authorized case and matches expected metadata before processing; do not trust the client completion signal.
- Avoid arbitrary remote document URLs. If remote retrieval is later required, add explicit SSRF protections; not needed for this MVP.
- Parameterized queries, output escaping, safe markdown rendering, strict security headers/CSP appropriate to the app, and a narrow CORS policy if separate origins are used.
- Do not store auth tokens, raw bank records or audio in ordinary localStorage. Cache the public app shell, not authenticated API responses or signed URLs in the service worker.
- Optional local drafts require explicit device-level consent, clear expiry and removal on logout/delete. Explain that browser local storage is not a secure vault, especially on shared devices. Default to memory-only private drafts and use server persistence when online.
- Collect only necessary data. Do not require Aadhaar or full bank statements. Advise users to crop unrelated personal/customer information.
- Raw audio should be deleted after confirmed transcription unless the user explicitly chooses to retain it and the retention path is implemented. Keep derived facts only as needed for the case.
- Define and disclose actual retention settings; do not invent a retention promise the code does not enforce. Account/case deletion must remove corresponding objects, facts, derived drafts and cached private data, with truthful explanation of provider backup limitations.
- AI-provider processing and retention settings must be checked before real personal data is enabled. Do not claim “never used for training” or “end-to-end encrypted” without a verified basis.
- Never enable session replay on private screens. Logs include operation IDs and error categories, not document contents, transcripts, OTPs or secrets.
- Use redacted synthetic fixtures in source control. Restrict exported packets to user-selected evidence. Exporting must never create a public URL by default.

Review current applicable privacy requirements for a public real-worker launch. State what remains for legal/security review. An unreviewed hackathon prototype should remain a private pilot or synthetic demo rather than claiming certification.

11. OFFLINE BEHAVIOR, PERFORMANCE AND RESILIENCE

Keep the main interface responsive on low-end devices. Use lightweight layouts and avoid unnecessary charts, animation, polling and client-side AI libraries.

- Lazy-load camera utilities, PDF/export modules and other heavy code only when needed.
- Compress derivatives without destroying text legibility; use thumbnails for lists. Preserve the submitted original separately.
- Cache immutable public assets and reviewed public guidance with its review date. Never cache private responses indiscriminately.
- Offline: show the app shell and cached public guidance; allow explicitly consented drafts and manual earnings entries. Show “Saved on this device,” “Waiting for connection,” “Uploading” and “Saved securely” as distinct states.
- AI cannot run offline in this architecture. Do not show a queued request as completed. Synchronize on reconnect and app reopen; do not rely solely on browser background-sync support.
- Deduplicate sync using client-generated operation IDs, server idempotency and expected revisions. Resolve conflicts explicitly rather than overwriting silently.
- No fake progress percentages. Use real stages: upload, read records, confirm details, check payment.
- Handle interrupted uploads, expired sessions, timeouts, quota exhaustion, provider failures and app reloads. Preserve safe input and offer a clear retry.
- Avoid unbounded serverless background tasks after the response closes. Keep provider work within measured host timeouts; use a durable job mechanism only if already available or justified. Persist operation state and resume/retry safely.
- Performance targets, to measure rather than claim: roughly under 250 KB compressed initial application JavaScript where practical, no unnecessary third-party trackers, fast interactive shell, and no large layout shifts. Report actual results and device/network conditions.
- External AI response time is variable. Set bounded timeouts and return an actionable failure rather than indefinite spinners.

12. STATES AND EDGE CASES THAT MUST BE DESIGNED

Cover empty, loading, partially complete, offline, success, recoverable failure and terminal failure states for every async feature.

Specific cases:
- Microphone denied, recording unsupported, noisy audio, ambiguous spoken amount and interrupted recording: typed fallback and editable transcript.
- Blurry, cropped, rotated, duplicate or conflicting screenshots: request correction; never invent a missing amount.
- Unsupported language: retain the content and explain supported options, not a fake translation.
- Offer from a different date/zone; only partial conditions visible; tier ambiguity: unknown eligibility.
- Worker gives a number that conflicts with a screenshot: preserve both and ask, rather than overwriting evidence.
- Negative payout, advance recovery, COD offset and prior-period correction: explain settlement semantics.
- Duplicate tap, timeout after a successful operation, stale browser tab and two devices editing: idempotency and revision conflict handling.
- Currency/date parsing errors, half-day work, zero versus unknown expenses and very large values: validation and tested domain behavior.
- No discrepancy found: explain the calculation respectfully and allow an evidence-backed request for clarification.
- Complaint draft changed after approval: mark it unapproved until reviewed again.
- User tries to access another user’s record or file: deny without data disclosure.
- Deletion partially fails: show truthful pending/failed deletion and retry; do not show “deleted” prematurely.
- AI unavailable: allow manual entries and deterministic calculations from confirmed facts; use a labeled factual-template draft if appropriate. Live demo must still include at least one genuine configured GenAI operation to satisfy the core requirement.

13. TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE GATES

Write focused tests for real failure risks; do not spend the window chasing arbitrary coverage percentages or snapshotting every component.

Unit tests:
- Integer money parsing/formatting and reconciliation, including COD and advance semantics.
- Incentive met/not_met/unknown, tier behavior, incomplete conditions and period/timezone boundaries.
- Daily-wage balance with half-days and advances.
- Unknown versus zero, duplicate records and invalid/out-of-range inputs.
- Schema validation, unsupported generated amounts, stale-result invalidation and complaint source references.

Integration tests:
- Authenticated owner can create/read/edit/delete; a second user cannot access the same case, child facts, operation, export or storage object.
- Inserts/updates cannot attach a child record to another owner’s case.
- Upload byte/type/size validation and unauthorized upload finalization.
- Real database migrations and RLS policies in an isolated test setup where available; do not describe mocked repository tests as verification of live RLS.
- Provider success, timeout, rate limit, malformed JSON, fabricated citation and prompt-injection text through controlled adapters.
- Same idempotency key returns the existing operation and does not duplicate persisted results/provider jobs; distinguish this from a provider charging for an unavoidable uncertain network retry.
- Revision conflicts and delete cleanup.

End-to-end tests with Playwright:
1. Hindi fictional incentive mismatch: select example, review extraction, correct a field, calculate, create/edit complaint and export/copy.
2. Matching payment: no false discrepancy or recovery claim.
3. Missing offer condition: information-needed result and no invented entitlement.
4. Login and private case save/reload, with real test authentication if available.
5. Daily-wage entry and balance.
6. Offline draft/reconnect without duplicate entries.
7. Keyboard flow, focus restoration and accessible errors; run axe on core screens.

Use provider fakes for repeatable automated tests, plus one explicitly reported live AI smoke test with synthetic content when credentials are available. Never invent successful test results when commands cannot run.

Manual QA:
- 320px and 390px layouts, desktop layout, 200% zoom and long Hindi text.
- Screen-reader/keyboard spot-check on intake, confirmation and results; document what was tested.
- Mobile camera/gallery permission behavior and export/share fallback.
- No overflow, obscured CTA, overlapping keyboard/bottom navigation or unreadable money values.
- Check console errors, failed network requests and production build.
- Measure initial bundle size and a throttled loading sample; avoid claiming real-device testing if only emulation was used.

Release gates:
- Lint, TypeScript check and production build pass.
- Critical domain and ownership tests pass.
- One core journey is verified end to end, including the honest uncertainty outcome.
- Real credentials are server-only and private uploads are not publicly reachable.
- No submitted/approved/recovered status is invented.
- Core screen text and errors are usable in Hindi and English.
- Any unimplemented supporting path is named in the final report; no dead “success” buttons.

14. SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA AND JUDGE STORY

Use a fictional provider called “Demo Delivery.” Watermark every fixture and sample view “Fictional demo data.” Never impersonate a real platform’s statement or present sample conditions as current platform policy.

Fixture A — possible shortfall:
- Offer: INR 600 incentive for at least 30 qualifying completed orders on the specified date in the specified zone/shift, no additional eligibility conditions in this fictional offer; payout due and settled in the same completed period.
- Activity: 32 qualifying orders with matching date/zone/shift.
- Incentive credited: INR 300; no separate adjustment or pending payment.
- Result: possible incentive difference INR 300, subject to the records confirmed.

Fixture B — correctly paid:
- Same offer, only 29 qualifying completed orders, no incentive credited.
- Result: threshold not met; no missing incentive established.

Fixture C — incomplete evidence:
- Screenshot omits relevant offer conditions or matching activity dates.
- Result: more information needed, named missing fields, no confident amount claimed.

Fixture D — settlement misunderstanding:
- Base earnings INR 1,000, earned incentive INR 200, cash retained INR 300, documented advance offset INR 100, bank settlement INR 800.
- Expected bank settlement = 1,000 + 200 - 300 - 100 = INR 800; no settlement difference.
- Separately recorded fuel expense INR 150 means earnings after this recorded expense are INR 1,050 before other unrecorded costs; do not equate this to cash available today or subtract the advance as an earnings loss.

Fixture E — daily-wage record:
- Eight confirmed days × INR 700 = INR 5,600; confirmed payments INR 2,000; recorded balance INR 3,600.
- Label any self-reported workday honestly; a ledger is not conclusive legal proof.

Two-minute live demo:
- Worker speaks the issue in Hindi or types it if voice is unavailable.
- Upload or select the synthetic records and run an actual GenAI extraction with the configured provider.
- Confirm facts, show the deterministic calculation and inspect the supporting evidence.
- Generate and review the complaint; show copy/print and saved case status.
- Switch to Fixture C to show the system declining to invent missing facts.
- Briefly show Earnings and sourced Help, demonstrating alignment with the wider problem statement.

If a provider is unavailable, label the separate fixture-extraction fallback “Recorded demo result.” Do not claim it was generated live. Keep this fallback isolated from live processing and explain the missing integration in the final report.

Use the time windows as checkpoints. Work in vertical slices and begin tests with the domain logic, not only at the end.

01:30–02:10 — Result screen, evidence view, complaint drafting/editing, copy/print, case save/status and integration tests.
02:10–02:40 — Earnings/daily-wage path, curated bilingual Help, voice if configured, safe offline draft behavior.
02:40–03:20 — Critical security/integration/E2E tests, accessibility checks, failure states and mobile layout fixes. Stop adding optional features.
03:20–04:00 — Production build, configured deployment, live smoke test, demo rehearsal and truthful handoff. Fix blockers before cosmetic refinements.

- Provide .env.example with variable names and explanations only. Separate public project identifiers from server secrets. Validate required config at startup or clearly at feature entry.

- Document rollback, backup/deletion limits, source-update responsibilities, known limitations and a small post-hackathon hardening list.

Required handoff files in the project:
